# Naming the Need: Three Tiers of Visibility

Surveys can only ask about what someone thought to put on the page. There are more needs in a community than any single survey instrument will catch. The City of Irving has polled a lot of people but it did it in a way that highlights what's already more visible.

The first tier is named and counted: needs that show up in closed-ended survey questions, where residents can point to something and a checkbox already exists for it. Homelessness, street and sidewalk maintenance, retail and dining variety, and mental health services all landed in this tier for South Irving.

The second tier is seen by institutions: needs that surface in stakeholder interviews and casework rather than resident checkboxes. Frontline staff and service providers already carry this knowledge, even when no resident survey asked about it directly. Substandard rental conditions, the mismatch between local wages and workforce, DART access, seniors being priced out, and the absence of any missing-middle housing all fall here.

The third tier is felt but not named: things that surface in open, unprompted conversation, with no neat survey category to hold them. Things you do not tell your government with your name attached. Concepts like comfort, necessary frugalities, the mental load of living in a neglected neighborhood. These needs shape where people will and will not go, whether or not anyone ever measured them.

This tiering is a companion to [[The Needs Gap - Needs vs. Known Needs]]. That note draws the line between a need and a known need. This one adds texture to the known side, showing that even recognized needs aren't recognized equally.
