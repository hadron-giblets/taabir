Taabir's three imagined functions, a resource center for vocational training, cooperative business, and the solidarity economy; a co-working space for member organizations and businesses; and a community space for those most in need, sort cleanly against the tiers laid out in [[Naming the Need - Three Tiers of Visibility]].

| Taabir function | What we found | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Vocational training, cooperative business, solidarity economy | Small South Irving businesses cannot retain workers at the wages they can offer. 20 percent of adults 25+ lack a high school diploma. 18.5 percent of Spanish speakers report limited English. Local businesses are largely small, locally owned survivors after chains left, exactly the base a cooperative or shared-purchasing model could formalize. | Seen by institutions |
| Co-working space for member orgs and businesses | Commercial building rehab is a top-3 named business priority (39 percent). Residents want run-down commercial space repurposed, not just replaced. ICAN already convenes 50+ organizations and has no fixed home mentioned in the plan, a possible anchor-tenant base. | Named and counted |
| Community space for those most in need | Mental health, senior services, and homelessness are the top 3 named community-service needs. Hidden, doubled-up homelessness is real but uncounted. Residents want a place that feels safe and untransactional; they are stressed. | Felt, not named |

Read against that tiering, the pattern is that Taabir's most concrete function, vocational training, maps to the tier least visible to residents themselves, while its most aspirational/soft function, the community space, maps to the tier that's hardest to count. So, is that a mismatch of priorities?
