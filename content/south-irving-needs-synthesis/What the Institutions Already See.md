title: "What the Institutions Already See"
---

The Consolidated Plan's stakeholder interviews, with service providers, small business owners, and school staff, surface a layer that resident surveys mostly miss.

On housing: South Irving is largely built out, with no multifamily construction since 1990, yet homes listing above $400,000 have started appearing in the same census tracts. New construction skews toward larger, pricier homes, leaving almost no missing-middle stock (townhomes, duplexes) and a specific shortage of larger rentals for working families. Seniors were named in every stakeholder consultation as the group most at risk of being priced out.

On workforce: small businesses in South Irving report open positions they cannot fill or retain, and the reason is wages, not a lack of interest. About 20 percent of Irving adults 25 and older do not have a high school diploma, and 18.5 percent of Spanish-speaking residents report limited English proficiency, both of which correlate directly with underemployment. The region's targeted industries, aerospace, healthcare, logistics, manufacturing, and tech, need a credentialed pipeline this population isn't yet connected to.

On transportation: DART is cutting bus routes in parts of the city, and the changes make service less reliable. One service provider has started driving a mobile unit out to clients rather than expecting them to reach her office. ISD staff report that these transit gaps directly block student access to internships.

Crisis response and casework, on the other hand, are relatively well covered already. Roughly 20 organizations are active in South and West Irving on this front: Irving Cares, church-run food pantries, Metrocare mental health, the Parkland-run Irving Health Center, WIC, the Family Advocacy Center, Brighter Tomorrows, and the Salvation Army, coordinated loosely through ICAN, a network of more than 50 member organizations. The city's own HOME-ARP public service need list names counseling, mental health treatment, dental care, banking services, secure storage, after-hours showers, laundry and hot meals, short-term rent assistance, housing navigation, job skills, life skills, and childcare, a useful inventory of what the city is already working on. Vocational training and business development services are not on that list.
