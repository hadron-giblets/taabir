# Session 6: Life Design

Life design means knowing your purpose and structuring your economic choices around it: divesting from harm, investing in community, reducing cash dependency, broadcasting your needs instead of hiding them.

That only works if you are allowed to have preferences in the first place, and preferences require believing you are allowed to want things. In a survival economy, you do not have the luxury of preferences. You just take what's available. This self-erasure, eliminating your own desires just to survive poverty, is the opposite of life design.

Under that lens, Taabir isn't only a community center. It is infrastructure for life design itself. The welcome room is where someone who's been told their preferences don't matter gets to start noticing what they actually want. The vocational school is where people practice being producers instead of only consumers. The cooperative incubator is where people get to build a livelihood around purpose rather than bare survival. Taabir serves the community, but the work happening inside any one person (figuring out what they actually want) is just as real as the institutional goals.

That is also why the welcome room comes first. The course's Hierarchy of Consumption framework assumes you already know your preferences, since the whole model runs on being able to ask "do I need this?" Taabir's first job is helping people recover those preferences before that question is even available to ask. Free and unconditional is not a nice feature of the welcome room. It is the precondition for everything else the building is meant to do.

The pace this runs on isn't geological (too slow to matter in a human life) or quarterly (too fast for a community to actually change). It's agricultural: plant, tend, wait, adjust, show up regardless of the weather. This is the practice of generational patience ([[Bylaws#Generational Patience]]), measuring success in seasons and decades rather than fiscal quarters. The site strategy's phased development, Phase 0 through Phase 4, is that pacing made architectural.

The bylaws' clause naming burnout as a structural failure, not a personal one, is the same logic applied to the people doing the work. You cannot hold a room open for other people if you are the one suffocating. Generational patience is not only an organizational value here. It is a life design value for the staff too.
