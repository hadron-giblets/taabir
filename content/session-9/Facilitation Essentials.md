# Facilitation Essentials

Without enough structure, power does not disappear from a group. It concentrates invisibly, usually in whoever is already most comfortable speaking up. Facilitation is what makes power visible and distributed on purpose, rather than left to default the informal way.

Good facilitation starts with knowing which kind of meeting is happening: information-sharing, problem-solving, or decision-making, since a room run as if it is making a decision when it is actually just sharing information produces frustration on both sides. An agenda held close but not tight, and a human check-in before diving into content, since connection is the foundation that makes efficient work possible afterward, not a delay before the real work starts.

A few facilitation phrases do a lot of work: "Thanks for your thoughts, could you clarify?" "Do I sense that you disagree with what was just said?" "I'm hearing two different needs, can we name them both?" The skill is interrupting without dismissing, and seeking clarity without turning it into an interrogation. Closing matters as much as opening: identify next steps, close with a round of highlights or "even better if," and end the meeting as a meeting rather than letting it dissolve slowly into fatigue.
