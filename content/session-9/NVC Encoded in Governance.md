# NVC Encoded in Governance

Nonviolent communication gives conflict a structure rather than leaving it to instinct, and Taabir's governance already encodes a version of that structure without naming it as NVC. See [[Nonviolent Communication (NVC)]] for the full framework.

A valid objection under the bylaws requires naming harm specifically: a decision would actively harm Taabir or a community it serves, violate a stated value, or create legal or financial risk. That's observation, need, and impact, built directly into how decisions get blocked, rather than left to whoever feels strongest about their preference in the moment. "Preferences are not objections" is NVC's distinction between a strategy and an underlying need, written straight into governance.

The transformative accountability process follows NVC's arc at organizational scale rather than individual scale: acknowledge the harm, do a root cause analysis, run a facilitated process that centers the person who was harmed, and make structural changes to prevent it recurring. None of that is punitive in intent. It's structural, the organizational equivalent of NVC's request step, aimed at a concrete change rather than an assignment of blame.

The vitality principle matters here too, in a very literal sense. A community center is going to gather people who hold strong, sometimes incompatible positions, about politics, parenting, faith, money. That intensity isn't a problem to manage down. It's the actual material of connection, if the people in the room are willing to do the translation work rather than just discharging the intensity at each other.

> **From the Bylaws (§V Consent-Based Decision Making):**
> Consent does not mean universal agreement. It means no one has a valid objection. A valid objection is one where the person reasonably believes the decision would: (1) actively harm TAABIR or a community it serves; (2) violate its stated values; or (3) create a legal or financial risk to the organization.

> **From the Bylaws (§V Transformative Accountability):**
> When harm occurs between members of the TAABIR community, the organization's first response shall be investigation and repair. Harm is understood as information about systemic failure, not merely individual failing. All parties involved in a harm event—including the person who caused harm, the person who experienced harm, and the community context—shall be considered in any accountability process.
