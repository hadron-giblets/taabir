title: "Nonviolent Communication (NVC)"
---

A four-step communication framework developed by Marshall Rosenberg, built on separating what happened from the interpretation of what happened, so a conversation can address an actual need instead of an accusation.

- **Observation:** state what happened in neutral, verifiable language, not an interpretation of someone's character. "Our last three meetings didn't have agendas" is an observation. "You're disorganized" is a judgment.
- **Feeling:** name the actual emotion, not a judgment disguised as one. "I feel frustrated" is a feeling. "I feel like you do not care" is a judgment smuggled in through "feel like."
- **Need:** identify the universal human need underneath the feeling, not a demand tied to one specific person or fix. Not "I need you to text back faster" (a strategy) but "I need reliability" (the need underneath it). Needs like safety, autonomy, connection, and order are shared by everyone, which is what makes them a starting point rather than a demand.
- **Request:** make a specific, doable ask. "Could we spend five minutes at the start of each meeting reviewing the agenda?" produces movement. "I want you to be more responsible" is too vague to act on.

Two ideas extend the four steps past a simple script. Vitality treats intensity, anger, or the urge to say something sharp, not as a malfunction to suppress but as energy pointing at a need that matters. Translating a judgment ("you are being a jerk") into its observation, feeling, and need does not remove the intensity. It redirects it toward a request instead of an attack. Self-connection holds that the process has to run inward before it runs outward: naming your own feeling and need first is what makes it possible to get curious about someone else's, including the more advanced move of guessing at another person's need without needing to be right about it, holding the guess as an offer rather than a diagnosis.

**Where it shows up in TAABIR's bylaws:** the consent process's definition of a valid objection, that a decision would actively harm TAABIR or a community it serves, violate a stated value, or create legal or financial risk, is observation, feeling, and need built directly into governance, rather than left to whoever feels most strongly about a preference. The transformative accountability process follows the same arc at organizational scale: acknowledge harm, root cause analysis, a facilitated process centering the person harmed, structural changes to prevent recurrence.
