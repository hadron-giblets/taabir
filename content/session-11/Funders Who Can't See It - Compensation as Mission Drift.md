# Funders Who Can't See It: Compensation as Mission Drift

Philanthropy is often reluctant to fund direct compensation for low-income community members, and that reluctance isn't a side issue for an organization whose actual work is paying people for their time and labor. The vocational school, the cooperative incubator, the community programs, all of it involves compensating people who do not usually get compensated for this kind of work. Some funders read that as mission drift, as if the "real" impact is somewhere else and the paychecks are overhead.

Taabir's position has to be the opposite of that read: compensation is the mission, not adjacent to it. The bylaws' living-wage floor and time-as-equity principle aren't administrative details sitting next to the impact. They are the impact, structurally encoded rather than left to discretion.

The practical implication is a qualifying question in reverse. Just as Taabir should qualify funders on their track record and expectations, it should also qualify them on whether they can actually see compensation as impact. A funder who cannot is not, for Taabir's purposes, the right funder, however well-resourced or well-intentioned they are.
