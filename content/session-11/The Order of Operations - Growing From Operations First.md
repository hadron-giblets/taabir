# The Order of Operations: Growing From Operations First

Venture capital structurally excludes most next economy enterprises, so the useful question isn't "how do we make ourselves fundable by investors" but "what order should we pursue funding in at all." The sequence: grow from operations first (pre-sales, revenue cushions, community certificates), then partner resources (better supplier terms, shared overhead), then non-dilutive funding (grants, program-related investments, mission-related investments, donor-advised funds), then community investment (crowdfunding, non-accredited investors, neighborhood capital), and only then professional investors, carefully qualified and on mission-protecting terms.

The logic isn't caution for its own sake. Every dollar carries an obligation, and the instruments earlier in the sequence generally carry lighter ones. Exhausting the cheaper, more autonomy-preserving options first means outside investment, when and if it is needed, arrives from a position of choice rather than desperation.

For an organization with no revenue yet, this order of operations still applies, just compressed to its first rung: can the community pre-invest before the thing it is investing in exists? Neighbors purchasing founding-member certificates redeemable for future services. A cooperative incubator's first cohort pre-paying for training that also funds the space itself. This is "grow from operations" applied to a pre-revenue organization: funding the build through commitment, not through outside capital.
