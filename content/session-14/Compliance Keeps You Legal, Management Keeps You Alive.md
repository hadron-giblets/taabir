# Compliance Keeps You Legal, Management Keeps You Alive

Compliance is what the state requires: taxes, filings, audits. Management is what the organization chooses for its own decision-making: dashboards, projections, variance analysis. Compliance keeps an organization legal. Management keeps it alive, and the two aren't independent of each other. Government grants carry compliance requirements, indirect cost rates, eligible expenses, documentation standards, that shape which management decisions are even possible in the first place.

Three financial orientations sit underneath both: the past, shown in the P&L statement, the present, shown in the balance sheet and cash position, and the future, shown in the operating projection. Most organizations that track their finances at all live almost entirely in the past and avoid the future, because forecasting can feel like fabrication rather than planning.

If Taabir takes on city funding or government grants, compliance requirements will shape operations directly: eligible expenses, documentation standards, indirect cost rate calculations, and reporting timelines all constrain what management decisions are actually available, what the space can be used for, who can be served, how time gets tracked. This is the "flavors" insight from the partnerships session applied to money specifically: city-funded operations require one posture, community-funded operations allow another, and the financial systems have to track both separately rather than blending them together.
