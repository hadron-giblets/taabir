Four ideas from this session: the difference between what the state requires and what the organization actually chooses for decisionmaking, the operating projection as the core tool for testing whether a plan survives contact with reality, cash flow visibility as a survival discipline rather than an obsessive habit, and open book management as infrastructure for shared power.

## The notes

**[[Compliance Keeps You Legal, Management Keeps You Alive]]** — Compliance is what the state requires; management is what the organization chooses.

**[[The Operating Projection as Strategic Tool]]** — A living model with an assumptions tab and a summary tab, built for "what if": raise wages, change the rent, lose a funder, and forecast the effects.

**[[Cash Flow as Survival Discipline]]** — Simple math with high stakes: beginning cash, expected in, expected out.

**[[Enough for Everybody - Open Book Management]]** — Universal access to financial information paired with literacy training to make it meaningful, because distributed information produces distributed problem-solving.
