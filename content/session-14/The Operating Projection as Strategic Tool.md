# The Operating Projection as Strategic Tool

The operating projection is the core strategic financial tool, a living model with two parts: an assumptions tab, price per unit, cost of goods per unit, wages, rent, monthly unit sales, and a summary tab showing monthly and annual totals against sustainability. The model exists to test "what if": raise wages, change the rent, add a service, lose a funder, and watch what ripples through the summary. What it reveals over time is which variables are actually sensitive, meaning which ones most determine whether the organization survives.

Forecasting with integrity means triangulating three things: historicals, what actually happened; reference points, what comparable organizations report; and goals, what the organization needs. The forecast is a reasoned estimate bounded by real data, not a fantasy, and conservative assumptions are appropriate whenever the cost of overestimating is severe. Variance analysis closes the loop: each month, compare what was projected to what actually happened, and treat the gap as information rather than failure. Over time the model gets more accurate, and the team learns which assumptions were sound and which need revising.

For Taabir, the operating projection is not a claim about what will happen. It's a structured question about what could happen, bounded by the evidence available right now. The site strategy imagines phased development, acquisition, retrofit, programming, expansion, and each phase carries its own costs and its own revenue. The operating projection is how the team tests whether a given phase is actually feasible: at this acquisition cost, with this grant support and this earned revenue, does the organization survive the transition? The model reveals what is sensitive, acquisition cost, grant timing, staffing levels, and that tells the team where to focus negotiation and fundraising energy rather than spreading it evenly across everything.
