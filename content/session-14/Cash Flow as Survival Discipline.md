# Cash Flow as Survival Discipline

Dashboards distill full financial statements down into five to ten numbers a team can actually discuss together: cash on hand, accounts receivable and payable, total income, direct costs, net result, impact metrics, compared against previous periods. The dashboard exists for conversation, and the question to ask of it is always "what story is this telling?"

Cash flow projection, done weekly or monthly, is simple arithmetic with high stakes: beginning cash plus expected income minus expected expenses equals ending cash. When ending cash approaches zero, the projection has already bought the organization time, which is the most valuable resource available in an actual crisis. Without it, the crisis gets discovered on the day payroll is already due, which is the worst possible moment to discover anything.

For a small organization with limited reserves, this is the most urgent financial practice available. One person spending fifteen to thirty minutes a week reviewing the cash position, what is in the account, what is expected in, what is owed, when payroll is due, is health. It's the minimum viable attention required so the organization does not discover insolvency exactly the day it's too late.
