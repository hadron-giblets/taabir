# Enough for Everybody: Open Book Management

Open book management means universal access to financial information paired with universal literacy training to make that access actually meaningful, since access without the ability to read it isn't really access. The reasoning: distributed information produces distributed problem-solving. A team that can see the cash flow can respond to a crisis together. A team that cannot see it can only panic when the crisis arrives.

With four team members, open book management at Taabir is close to natural already, since everyone already knows the financial situation informally just from being in the room. The work is making that explicit rather than assumed: a monthly review where the team looks at the dashboard together, understands the cash position together, and makes collective decisions about priorities from a shared picture rather than four separate guesses. This is what the bylaws' transparency commitment looks like in practice, not just on paper.

The financial system's purpose, at bottom, is ensuring there is enough for everybody: the team is compensated, the space is sustained, and the mission continues. It isn't to maximize surplus or to impress funders with growth trajectories. The bylaws' 2:1 pay ratio cap and living-wage floor are the values. The financial system is how the team verifies, in practice and not just in principle, that those values are actually being met.
