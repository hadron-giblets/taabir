# Research as Ownership: The Needs Assessment Reframe

Business-as-usual tools like positioning matrices, segmentation, and market research aren't inherently extractive. A positioning matrix is just a way of mapping where an industry sits on the axes that matter to the people it serves. What determines whether the tool does harm is the posture behind it: collaborative analysis (who else serves this community, and how might we complement them?) versus competitive analysis (how do we beat the other options?).

The Build-Measure-Learn loop, hypothesis, cheap experiment, measure, learn, revise, works the same way. It's a discipline for testing before committing capital, not a mandate to treat community members as test subjects. The discipline is in building cheap: a landing page and a way to gauge real interest, not a finished program no one asked for.

Taabir's team felt the Community Needs Assessment as "corporate," and that reaction was picking up on something real. When research treats people as data sources, what can we learn from you, it replicates extraction even with the best intentions. When it treats people as future co-owners, what could we build together, it becomes relationship-building instead. The assessment wasn't market research in the conventional sense. It was the first act of community ownership, framed as a survey.
