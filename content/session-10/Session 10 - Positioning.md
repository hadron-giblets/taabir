Four ideas from this session: the gap between what people need and what they know they need, why research posture matters as much as research content, how choosing a specific audience is different from narrowing who gets served, and how to find the open space in a crowded field of existing options.

## The notes

**[[The Needs Gap - Needs vs. Known Needs]]** — A need exists whether or not it's articulated, but trust is built by serving people's known needs.

**[[Research as Ownership - The Needs Assessment Reframe]]** — Positioning matrices, segmentation, and build-measure-learn aren't inherently extractive; what decides the question is whether research treats people as data sources or as future co-owners.

**[[Same Product, Different Door - Segmentation and Value Proposition]]** — "Everyone in Irving" is a category, not an audience.

**[[The Free and Relational Quadrant - Positioning Matrix]]** — Plotting services on some axes: expensive or free? Transactional or relational?
