title: "The Needs Gap: Needs vs. Known Needs"
---

A need exists whether or not the people experiencing it recognize it as something addressable. A known need is one people are actively looking to solve. The gap between the two produces the situation where an organization builds something and no one comes. The bridge across that gap isn't a better product. It's education, trust, and meeting people where they actually are, not where a program officer imagines them to be.

Tim Wise's framing puts it plainly: a neighborhood knew it needed a stop sign before it needed an anti-racism organizer. Build the stop sign first. Trust gets built by visibly solving the problem someone can already name, and that trust is what eventually makes room for the conversation about the problem they couldn't yet name.

Taabir sees a need clearly: a free, welcoming place to belong. Whether South Irving already recognizes that need in those terms is a separate question. The community conversations and canvassing are the bridge. People are more likely to say "somewhere for the kids" or "a place to sit" than "belonging" or "connection." The positioning work isn't correcting that language. It's building toward it, trusting that the deeper need gets met through whatever specific door the community actually opens.
