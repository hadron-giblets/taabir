This session is different from the ones before it. Where earlier sessions taught frameworks, this one is built almost entirely from personal narrative, four instructors' accounts of how they arrived at this work and their conclusions on why any of it matters. The content here does not decompose into discrete principles the way earlier sessions did. It's one argument.

LIFT Economy's theory of change moves across several registers at once: consulting done one-on-one, the MBA reaching many people at a time, a capital fund, hundreds of hours of storytelling through podcasts, published books, and broader field-building work. The systems change themes underneath all of it: moving away from extraction toward circularity, measuring progress by wellbeing rather than growth, keeping supply chains local, shifting from private ownership toward shared stewardship, and reducing how much of daily life has to run through money at all. None of these change on their own. The course's premise is that the economy cannot transform without the systems around it, governance, education, law, and the deeper assumptions people carry about how the world works, changing alongside it.

The four instructors each described a turning point where crisis or disillusionment opened into something else.

Phoenix: Haitian immigrant. Neighborhood with trauma. Activist communities that could diagnose the problem but could not get along with each other. Demoralization. Then: meditation. [[Nonviolent Communication (NVC)]]. The discovery that you can change yourself. The admission that this work takes enormous time and privilege, managed in four-day work weeks, three-month retreats. And the question: can I make the path shorter for others?

Aaron: Laid off from BALLE (now Common Future). The layoff was a gift. Onboarding at LIFT was informal, an invitation that said "let's collaborate." Over time: questioning the assumption that salary must increase every year. War tax resistance. A $50,000 off-grid tiny house on a cooperatively run school, bartering housing for community labor. Life as experiment in reducing reliance on financial currency.

Kevin: Youth spent angry about the state of the world. With his friends, he planned to make movies to show people what is wrong, and they'll change. Grew up and raised millions in tech. Hired hundreds of people. Bought a house. Realized he'd never made the documentary. Depression. Then, he sold everything, went to live on a farm, learned permaculture. The shift from seeing crisis everywhere to seeing opportunity everywhere. "So much more than enough for everybody." Students asking: "I love food forests, but I hate my job. What do I do?" That question birthed LIFT Economy.

Sean: Studied nuclear physics. Discovered the research was bomb research, funded through Department of Defense money laundered through the Department of Energy. Walked out. "I'm going to do something beautiful with my life." Started a woodworking cooperative. Took 13 years to figure out. The lesson: it shouldn't take this long. Pattern: always oriented toward circles like sports teams, yoga societies, men's groups, cooperatives. Building mutual benefit through collective structure.

None of these paths were linear or planned, and all of them required real privilege, time, education, some baseline safety, alongside real pain. The instructors are upfront about that: the path isn't equally accessible to everyone. What they hope to offer is a shorter version of it for the people coming after them.
