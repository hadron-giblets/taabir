# The Everyone Paradox: Personas and Specificity

Personas are research-grounded fictional profiles, not just demographics but life circumstances, values, information channels, and needs. They're a tool for empathy, not for targeting: the persona helps a team ask where this person already goes and what they'd actually respond to. Multiple personas are normal, and audience research is the process of building them by listening more than talking, asking what a person's life is like, what they value, how they get information, and how their needs are or aren't currently being met. The Seedles lesson from an earlier session applies directly here: the product a team imagines isn't always the product people actually want. Listen for the surprise rather than confirming what you already expected to hear.

Taabir is oriented toward Irving, but "everyone in Irving" is a category, not an audience, and a space designed for everyone often ends up serving no one well, because no one recognizes themselves in it. The real question isn't who could use Taabir. It's who is the person for whom Taabir would be the answer. Maybe a young parent in West Irving with nowhere free to take their kid. Maybe someone recently housed who needs stability and connection. Maybe a teenager who needs wifi and somewhere that isn't school or home. Maybe a day laborer between gigs, or someone who just moved to Irving and does not know anyone yet. Possibly all of these, but the welcoming room needs to speak to someone specific first, and stay open enough that everyone else can still recognize themselves in it too.

That specificity has to extend to place, not just person. West Irving is not a generic neighborhood with generic needs. The audience research question isn't what communities generally need. It's what is actually missing within walking distance of the site Taabir is pursuing. The answer will be specific, maybe a place to sit, maybe English classes, maybe somewhere for elderly people to gather during the day, and that specificity is itself the positioning, not a narrowing of it.
