# A Message in Their Own Words

Messaging evolves through testing in real conversations, not through a single polished round of copywriting. The discipline is distilling the value proposition down to a few words and letting the community's own language inform that distillation rather than imposing an organization's internal vocabulary onto it. A member testimonial, "why I'm a member," tends to land as more authentic than any crafted tagline, because it is already in the language the audience uses with each other.

The site strategy's own framing, three things at once: resource center, co-working space, community space, is accurate but not magnetic. It describes Taabir without making anyone want to walk in. The magnetic version of that message hasn't been written yet, and it will not come from a brainstorm. It will emerge from hearing what people actually say they need, in their own words, the same way the audience research already happening on porches and in community rooms will eventually surface it. A testimonial along the lines of "Taabir is like ___ for me" will do more work than any tagline a team could invent on its own.
