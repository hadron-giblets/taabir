# Alignment-Seeking, Not Broadcasting

Marketing, reframed, isn't extraction or manipulation. It's alignment-seeking: finding the people who already need what an organization offers and helping them find their way to it. The question shifts from "how do we make people want this" to "who already wants this, and how do we reach them." That shift changes which channels actually matter. 

Word of mouth produces the highest return on effort of any channel, even though it reaches the smallest audience at a time. Strategic partnerships come next, then social media and PR, with paid advertising producing the lowest return despite reaching the largest audience. For an organization built on relationship rather than transaction, word of mouth is the anchor, not a nice-to-have supplement to paid channels.

Taabir grows and develops and is defined by conversation right now. It needs to keep building two-way channels. 

The Community Needs Assessment was audience research, even if it felt more distant than a direct conversation. The community gatherings where we discussed governance and bylaws and organizational design were audience research. Every porch conversation, every conversation at a mosque or a bar, is the word-of-mouth campaign already in motion if we tend these relationships and link them to a service people want to return to.
