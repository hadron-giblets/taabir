# Principle 10: Supporting Personal Growth and Development

In the business as usual economy, the workplace is a site of **production**. People are hired for a specific set of skills to produce a specific output. Growth is seen as something the employee does on their own time (or for the benefit of their resume), and burnout is treated as an individual weakness or a lack of resilience.

In a Solidarity Economy, the workplace is a site of **human development**. It acknowledges that no one arrives at a job fully formed and that the way we work together can actually help us grow emotionally and mentally.

Taabir integrates this into its operational policy:
- **Transformative Accountability:** When harm occurs, the response is investigation and repair rather than immediate punishment. It treats harm as a signal of systemic failure, turning a crisis into a learning opportunity ([[Bylaws#V. Accountability]]).
- **Structural Care:** Taabir explicitly defines burnout as a "structural failure, not an individual weakness" ([[Bylaws#Generational Patience]]). This shifts the burden from the exhausted individual to the system that broke them.

**In Practice:**
Personal growth is built into the "rules" of the organization:
- **The Consent Process:** Learning to identify and voice a "valid objection" to someone's proposed motion is a practical exercise in emotional intelligence and boundary setting.
- **The Learning Curve:** By treating the workplace as a site of growth, Taabir invests in the people doing the work, recognizing that a healthy, growing human is more valuable to the mission than a perfectly efficient but burnt-out worker.

I think there's a whole universe of principles and strategies and implementation to this concept. To creating an environment that serves the worker as much as the community. It's not wrong to want that, but it's very important to be honest and balance that as this org builds.
