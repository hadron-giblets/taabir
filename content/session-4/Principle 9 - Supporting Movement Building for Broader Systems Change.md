title: "Principle 9: Supporting Movement Building"
---

In the standard nonprofit model, organizations often compete for the same grants and focus on scaling their own specific program. This can lead to a fragmented landscape where organizations are more concerned with their own survival than the systemic cause of the problems they are fighting.

**Movement building** is about playing a bigger game. Instead of just providing a service, the organization uses its position to shift how power is organized in the entire region. The goal is to make the *solidarity model* so effective that other institutions, even city governments, begin to adopt similar practices.

Taabir builds this strategic posture into these parts of the bylaws:
- **Community-Led Governance:** The General Circle includes community representatives, ensuring the organization remains an extension of the neighborhood, not a provider to the neighborhood ([[Bylaws#Participatory Governance]]).
- **Anti-Accumulation:** The bylaws' dissolution clause requires remaining assets to go to 501(c)(3) organizations, with a stated preference for the solidarity economy movement — worker cooperatives, community land trusts, nonprofits with demonstrated commitment to participatory governance and mutual aid — and never to private individuals ([[Bylaws of Taabir USA#XIX. Dissolution]]).
- **Power Checks:** Using consent-based decision making and redundancy audits prevents power from concentrating internally, mirroring the same power-shifts Taabir wants to see in the city ([[Bylaws#V. Accountability]]).

**In Practice: The Community Connector**
The Community Connector role is the primary engine for this. Rather than traditional "outreach," this role focuses on embedding Taabir in South Irving and partnering with the City of Irving on a more involved model of service delivery based on partnership rather than a funder-grantee dynamic.
