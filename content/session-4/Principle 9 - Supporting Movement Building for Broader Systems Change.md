# Principle 9: Supporting Movement Building

In the standard nonprofit model, organizations often compete for the same grants and focus on "scaling" their own specific program. This can lead to a fragmented landscape where organizations are more concerned with their own survival than the systemic cause of the problems they are fighting.

**Movement Building** is about "Playing a Bigger Game." Instead of just providing a service, the organization uses its position to shift how power is organized in the entire region. The goal is to make the *solidarity model* so effective that other institutions (like city government) begin to adopt similar practices.

Taabir builds this strategic posture into its DNA:
- **Community-Led Governance:** The General Circle includes community representatives, ensuring the organization remains an extension of the neighborhood, not a provider *to* the neighborhood ([[Bylaws#Participatory Governance]]).
- **Anti-Accumulation:** The dissolution clause ensures that if Taabir ever closes, its assets don't go to another nonprofit, but to worker cooperatives and community land trusts.
- **Power Checks:** Using consent-based decision making and redundancy audits prevents power from concentrating internally, mirroring the same power-shifts Taabir wants to see in the city ([[Bylaws#V. Accountability]]).

**In Practice: The Community Connector**
The Community Connector role is the primary engine for this. Rather than traditional "outreach," this role focuses on embedding Taabir in West Irving and partnering with the City of Irving on a new model of service delivery—one based on partnership rather than a funder-grantee dynamic.
