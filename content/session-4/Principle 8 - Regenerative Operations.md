# Principle 8: Regenerative Operations

Most green initiatives in the standard economy are about **sustainability**, which often just means "doing less harm" (for example, reducing carbon footprints or using recycled paper). It treats the environment as a resource to be managed so it doesn't run out.

**Regenerative operations** go a step further. The goal isn't just to reduce damage, but to actively heal the ecosystem. It assumes that human activity can be reciprocal, that the act of running a business can actually leave the land and community healthier than it was before.

Taabir's bylaws prioritize practices that minimize harm and prefer vendors aligned with "voluntary simplicity and ecological sustainability" ([[Bylaws#Ecological Commitment]]).

**In Practice: The Building as a Textbook**
Taabir doesn't just operate in a space; it can use the physical infrastructure to teach regenerative stewardship in stages:
1. **Water Stewardship:** Implementing rainwater harvesting. This transforms water from a commodity to be bought into a resource to be stewarded.
2. **Healthy Materials:** Using straw bale insulation, natural clay plasters, and low-VOC paints to prioritize the health of the people inside.
3. **Embedded Education:** The vocational school doesn't just teach regenerative construction in a classroom. It can practice it on the actual building.

This brings up questions with renting a space vs. ownership. Landlords give a deal if you improve a space; that's a benefit for them and also just generally improves the community space. At the same time, Taabir wants to be anchored in its space and community and not risk losing it to increased rents or imposed restrictions. We need to work towards greater specificity of what this space really looks like, what is permanent and what is meant to be made of the cumulative work we do for Taabir.
