# Principle 7: Transparency

In traditional organizations, information is often used as a tool of **Control**. Knowledge is siloed at the top (the "black box" of management) to maintain a hierarchy of power. This often creates a culture of anxiety where staff feel unsafe because they do not actually know the health or direction of the organization.

In a Solidarity Economy, information is a tool for **Distributed Power**. For people to make meaningful decisions, they need the same facts as the leadership. Transparency isn't about surveillance; it's about creating psychological safety through trust.

Taabir implements this through several structural guards:
1. **Decoupling Knowledge from Authority:** The "Redundancy of Critical Functions" ([[Bylaws#Redundancy of Critical Functions]]) ensures that no single person is the sole holder of essential financial or operational knowledge.
2. **Participatory Budgeting:** Through the Financial Management Team, budget information is made accessible to staff, turning financial health into a shared community concern rather than a private management secret.

**In Practice:**
- **The Redundancy Audit:** The General Circle conducts an annual audit to ensure no "single points of failure" exist in the organization's knowledge base.
- **Honest Positioning:** When meeting with city stakeholders, Taabir is transparent about its constraints and what it *hasn't* figured out yet. This builds deeper trust than pretending to be a finished "solution."
