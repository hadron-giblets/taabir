title: "Principle 7: Transparency"
---

In traditional organizations, information is often used as a tool of **control**. Knowledge is siloed at the top (management decides what you need to know) to maintain a hierarchy of power. This often creates a culture of anxiety where staff feel unsafe because they do not actually know the health or direction of the organization. And frustratingly often, they're purposely misled by those up the hierarchy.

In a Solidarity Economy, information is a tool for **distributed power**. For people to make meaningful decisions, they need the same facts as the leadership. Transparency isn't about surveillance, though it can be alarming when we're used to a paranoid environment. Rather, it's about creating psychological safety through trust, and it's the only way to heal from that societal anxiety.

Taabir implements this through several structural guards:
1. **Decoupling Knowledge from Authority:** The "Redundancy of Critical Functions" ([[Bylaws#Redundancy of Critical Functions]]) ensures that no single person is the sole holder of essential financial or operational knowledge.
2. **Participatory Budgeting:** Through the Financial Management Team, budget information is made accessible to staff, turning financial health into a shared community concern rather than a private management secret.

**In Practice:**
- **The Redundancy Audit:** The General Circle conducts an annual audit to ensure no "single points of failure" exist in the organization's knowledge base.
- **Honest Positioning:** When meeting with community and city stakeholders, Taabir is transparent about its constraints and what it *hasn't* figured out yet. This builds deeper trust than pretending to be a finished "solution."
