# Tasks, Not Titles: Task-Based Design and Trust

Org structure can be creative, but it has to be clear enough to catch everything the organization actually does, assign each piece to a person, and hold that person accountable for it. Task-based design defines all of an organization's functions, groups them into thematic areas, design (vision, culture, strategy, operations), revenue (delivery), and admin (support), and then defines each person's role as a specific set of tasks within those areas rather than a title sitting above other titles. Accountability runs to the task, not to a manager, which makes evaluation granular (how's task fourteen going?) and makes reassignment possible without it reading as a judgment on anyone's worth.

Trust and structure are independent variables. High trust with hierarchy produces a functional traditional organization. Low trust with flat structure produces a suspicious collective that cannot actually move. High trust with flat structure is the next economy ideal. Low trust with hierarchy is corporate dystopia. Structure does not produce trust, and trust does not produce structure. Both have to be built on purpose, at the same time.

Taabiris guided by its project plans, a compilation of all the tasks we want to accomplish in a given 6 month period and what we need to focus on in the broad swaths. That focus has to sharpen as the work itself gets more defined, into yearly, quarterly, monthly, and daily plans. The Board Decision Matrix defines which decisions belong to which body, with the one necessary exception of legal liability, where someone's authority may need to supersede another's. Outside of that exception, both the community and the team itself keep pushing against hierarchies of power/advocating for accountable ownership.
