title: "The Redundancy of Care: Lead-Support and Personal Sustainability"
---

For any critical task, designating a lead with primary responsibility and a support person as backup and trainee prevents a single point of failure, and documentation ensures the institutional memory survives even when a person eventually leaves. This is a structural answer to a structural risk: an organization that depends entirely on one person's undocumented knowledge is one departure away from losing it.

Personal sustainability is the same logic applied to the people rather than the tasks. Burnout kills more organizations than funding gaps do. Rest has to be scheduled the way meetings are scheduled, not left to whatever time happens to be left over. Taabir's bylaws naming burnout as a structural failure rather than a personal one is a direct application of this: if the organization's design assumes people will eventually burn out and treats that as an individual weakness, the design itself is the problem, not the person who burns out inside it. A redundancy audit that only covers tasks and skips the people doing them hasn't actually covered the risk.
