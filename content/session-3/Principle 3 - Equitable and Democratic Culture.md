# Principle 3: Equitable and Democratic Culture

**The Thesis:** Decision-making power is distributed rather than concentrated. Equity here does not mean treating everyone identically; it means matching what people get to what they actually need. This is achieved through consent-based decision making, delegated authority, and sociocracy.

**The Bylaw:** Consent does not mean universal agreement. It means no one has a valid objection ([[Bylaws#Consent-Based Decision Making]]). The Board Decision Matrix draws a clear line between what staff decide alone, what needs staff-board consultation, and what the Board holds exclusively ([[Bylaws#VI. Decision Matrix]]).

**The Application:** The bylaws protect a democratic organization. The annual redundancy audit exists specifically so power does not quietly end up with one person. The Board can pause a decision but cannot veto it outright. Equity shows up in its most recognizable way in the pay structure: not equal pay, which would ignore that people have different needs, but pay constrained within a defined range, with a living wage floor and hardship exceptions.
