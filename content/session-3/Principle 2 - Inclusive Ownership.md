title: "Principle 2: Inclusive Ownership"
---

Economic rights (profits, proceeds from a sale) should be widely held rather than concentrated. Ownership does not have to be the same thing as governance; you can separate who has a stake in the outcome from who makes the day-to-day decisions.

**The Bylaw:** Time counts as equity, with unpaid hours recorded as community investment ([[Bylaws#Time as Equity Contribution]]). A 2:1 cap limits the gap between the highest and lowest paid employee ([[Bylaws#VII. Employee Compensation]]). On dissolution, remaining assets go to 501(c)(3) organizations with a stated preference for the solidarity economy movement — worker cooperatives, community land trusts — rather than back to founders ([[Bylaws of Taabir USA#XIX. Dissolution]]).

**The Application:** Taabir distributes ownership, and it redefines what counts as a stake. An hour of work becomes a claim on the organization's future regardless of credentials. The dissolution clause ensures that even the organization's end returns value to the community rather than individuals. This is designed so no one can extract from this structure, and nobody can hoard it.
