# Principle 2: Inclusive Ownership

**The Thesis:** Economic rights (profits, proceeds from a sale) should be widely held rather than concentrated. Ownership does not have to be the same thing as governance; you can separate who has a stake in the outcome from who makes the day-to-day decisions.

**The Bylaw:** Time counts as equity, with unpaid hours recorded as community investment ([[Bylaws#Time as Equity Contribution]]). A 2:1 cap limits the gap between the highest and lowest paid employee ([[Bylaws#VII. Employee Compensation]]). The dissolution clause sends any remaining assets to [[Solidarity Economy|solidarity economy]] institutions rather than back to founders (this clause is not yet present in the bylaws and should be added).

**The Application:** Taabir does not just distribute ownership; it redefines what counts as a stake. An hour of work becomes a claim on the organization's future regardless of credentials. The dissolution clause ensures that even the organization's end returns value to the community rather than to whoever happened to found it. Nobody can extract from this structure, and nobody can hoard it.
