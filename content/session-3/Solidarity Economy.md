The solidarity economy is a set of structural alternatives to extractive economics. Rather than organizing economic activity around the maximization of profit or the accumulation of capital, a solidarity economy organizes activity around the sustainability of the community and the meeting of human needs.

The transition to a "Next Economy" is a shift in economic design. In an extractive economy, the goal is capital accumulation (making the money grow). In a solidarity economy, the goal is social reproduction (making the community resilient, healthy, and sustainable).

## Structural Mapping: Extractive $\rightarrow$ Solidarity

To move from theory to practice, we can map specific extractive failures to their structural alternatives:

| Extractive Failure | Structural Alternative | Taabir Application |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Value Extraction**: Profits are siphoned away from workers and the neighborhood to distant shareholders. | **Inclusive Ownership**: Economic rights are widely held. Ownership is separate from governance. | Time is an equity investment, ensuring those who build the organization have a stake in its future. |
| **Speculative Displacement**: Land and resources are treated as assets for speculation, driving up costs and pushing out residents. | **Community Stewardship**: Removing essential resources like land from the speculative market. | Operating as a resource center for underserved groups to anchor local stability, and offering a local-focused place of commerce. |
| **Competitive Erasure**: Organizations compete for the same limited grants/attention, weakening the overall ecosystem. | **Abundance Practice**: Shifting from a scarcity mindset to one of collective sufficiency. | A commitment not to compete against peer organizations for grants when collaboration is possible. |
| **Concentrated Power**: Decision-making is top-down; those most affected by the work have the least say in how it's done. | **Participatory Governance**: Power is distributed to those with the most direct experience of the issues. | Use of consent-based decision making. |

## Summary
The litmus test for any practice within this framework is: **Does this activity meet people's needs, or does it extract value from them?**
