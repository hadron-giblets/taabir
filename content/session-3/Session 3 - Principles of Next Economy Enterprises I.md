LIFT Economy's ten principles for next-economy enterprises, part one (principles 1–5). [[Session 4 - Principles of Next Economy Enterprises II|Part two]] covers principles 6–10.

This is the session where the broader "what is the next economy" question from Sessions 1–2 turns into something testable: criteria you can run any organization through, Taabir included. Part one covers the first five: what an enterprise is for, who owns it, and how it decides.

## The notes

**[[Principle 1 - Need-Oriented Basic Goods and Services|Principle 1: Need-Oriented Basic Goods & Services]]** — Communities define their own needs. The community didn't ask for a vocational school; they asked for a place — and the first thing Taabir offers is free.

**[[Principle 2 - Inclusive Ownership]]** — Economic rights widely held rather than concentrated: time counts as equity, a 2:1 pay cap holds the range, and the dissolution clause gives even the organization's ending back to the movement.

**[[Principle 3 - Equitable and Democratic Culture]]** — Consent means no valid objection, not universal agreement. The Decision Matrix and the annual redundancy audit are the structural guards against power quietly pooling.

**[[Principle 4 - Supporting Local Economy Ecosystem]]** — Money that stays local keeps circulating, and even locally owned businesses can extract — the real decisions are procurement and banking. Taabir structurally refuses to compete against peers for grants.

**[[Principle 5 - Education Embedded in Goods and Services]]** — Teach people to do what you do. Success isn't Taabir closing; it's Taabir becoming one node in a city-wide network of cooperative spaces.

**[[Solidarity Economy]]** — The session's reference note: the structural map from extractive failure to solidarity alternative, with the Taabir application for each row.
