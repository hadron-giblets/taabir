Southern Environmental Law Center's core insight is that a nonprofit can be restructured to function like a cooperative without changing its legal status: the Board delegates, the workers decide, and the shell stays a 501(c)(3) while the actual operating culture is democratic.

Taabir is already built this way. It is a worker self-directed nonprofit where the General Circle holds operational power and the Board's role is stewardship rather than control. "The people who do the work should decide" is the defining idea behind worker self-direction, and it's not just a value statement here, it's how the organization is actually structured.

There's also a fiscal sponsorship involved: Taabir USA operates as a fiscally sponsored project of TAABIR, a U.S. 501(c)(3) with roots in Taabir Pakistan ([[Fiscal Sponsorship Agreement]]). That gives it a charitable shell without the administrative weight of incorporating independently, room to move fast and fit structure to function for now, with the option to spin off into independent incorporation later if the work demands it.

Right now, a single General Circle holds all decision-making power, which makes sense for a small team operating on high trust. But the site strategy already imagines several distinct functions (the welcoming room, the vocational school, the café, the cooperative incubator, the performance space), and as those mature, they'll likely need different governance structures from each other:

- The welcoming room stays with the nonprofit. It's free, not a business, so there's no ownership question to resolve.
- The vocational school could remain a program of the nonprofit, or become its own entity: a training cooperative where instructors and students share governance.
- The café could spin off into a worker-owned cooperative, owned by the people who operate it daily, with Taabir USA possibly retaining a partial equity stake (the Evergreen model a way for a nonprofit to hold ownership to ensure the business stays mission-aligned rather than being sold for profit) to keep mission alignment.
- The cooperative incubator is the engine that spawns new entities, and might itself end up structured as a multistakeholder co-op: Taabir, the new co-ops it births, and community investors all holding a piece.
- Worker housing, eventually, could take the form of a limited-equity housing cooperative or a land trust.

Zoom out, and Taabir USA functions as the gravitational center of a small ecosystem: the nonprofit that holds the mission, runs the welcoming room, and teaches people how to build cooperatives. The entities it helps create orbit around it, each autonomous, each democratically governed, each still connected back through relationship, shared services, or partial equity.

One asterisk on "actually structured": under the sponsorship agreement, worker self-direction is delegated — granted by the sponsor, terminable on ninety days' notice ([[Fiscal Sponsorship Agreement#3. Delegated Governance and Management]]). The democracy is real, and it is on loan. [[Three Governance Documents]] maps the whole arrangement.

"The people who do the work should decide" is a governance philosophy and a guide to how structure gets generated over time. When someone at Taabir starts new work, launching a program or building a business, they should come to own that work. Taabir doesn't hold onto the models it develops. It gives them to the workers and to the community. That's [[Principle 5 - Education Embedded in Goods and Services|Education Embedded]] and [[Principle 6 - Open Source and Regionally Replicable|Open Source]] built as part of the whole org structure.
