Taabir's planning conversations are really about one question: **what's the free version of this?** Free meaning more than no cost, but definitely at least no cost. No cost is the removal of the biggest gate this society imposes: access. 

Paying for a service is joining a system. Government services cost documents. Nonprofit services cost some kind of story as proof in the intake form. Free with no questions is the only modality that can accept you as you are, no matter who you are. For immigrants who don't want to pay for a service they don't believe in, for people who have learned that institutions extract more than they give, that's the precursor to trust.

So, let's talk about all the things Taabir can be.

## A certified kitchen

The bottleneck between half the home cooks in South Irving and a legal food business is a health permit, and a health permit is mostly about how you design a room: three-compartment sink, certified surfaces, inspection. All the things you need so you can get the *insurance* you need.
 
[La Cocina](https://www.lacocinasf.org/theory-of-change) in San Francisco built that room for women, immigrants and people of color in 2005 and has incubated award-winning chefs. Really, click the link. Their Theory of Change casts a huge net and their org has a huge impact.

[CommonWealth Kitchen](https://commonwealthkitchen.org/our-approach/theory-of-change/) does it in Boston. [The Hatchery](https://thehatcherychicago.org/who-we-are/#our_mission) does it in Chicago. 

Think about DFW, the proliferation of great food in this metroplex and unique fusion cuisine and the celebration of a land that is fertile. The great mix of people here we could gather in a shared certified kitchen. Something rentable by the hour, cheap for members and yielding them a profit (you can see already how we have to think about honoring "free" in multiple ways), big enough to run a catering co-op inside it. 

## A permanent shelf

Right now, if you're making something at home and want to sell it, you have to hit the event circuit. A nice afternoon at the farmer's market plus the table fees, weekends, weather out of hell DFW experiences much of the year, load-outs at midnight, and no accumulation because you sell only when you're standing there. 

But a consignment shelf is compounding presence. Your salsa can sell on Tuesday while you're at work, without another market fee or a weekend spent behind a table. Taabir takes a transparent cut and the money stays in the neighborhood. This is the nearest of all the dreams and built into our site planning, Phase 1 retail, the thing that pays for the free room.

## Building materials as curriculum

Straw bale construction is a century-old Nebraska tradition with houses still standing. Hempitecture is pressing hempcrete insulation in Idaho. 

The vocational school could teach regenerative building by making the materials Taabir's own building could use — imagine anything from rainwater recapture to innovations in insulation — and sell the surplus. A vision where students learn by producing and the building gets built out of its own coursework.

## A black box theatre

This is the cheapest kind of theatre you can have. A room painted black with a light grid. Lowering barrier for access to the arts and offering more options to the community, though it is perhaps a vanity: shoutouts to [MainStage Irving-Las Colinas](https://mainstageirving.com/about-us/) for offering diverse and inclusive and affordable theatre to the area and the Lochwood Branch Library for offering rentable black box theatre space for just a $30 fee.

## A room for religious economics

No-interest lending is the *oldest* financial technology. Riba is haram; the qard hasan, the benevolent loan, is a named instrument in Islamic finance. Medieval canon law cursed usury up and down. Hebrew free-loan societies have operated in American cities for 100+ years. 

And note the scale! The LDS church runs a welfare system that is among the most functional mutual aid in the country, the 130+ bishops' storehouses across the world. Their programs offer food and household goods for free, and the church owns welfare facilities like canneries, milk processing plants, grain silos and more to sustain a deep support network. Likewise, the Desjardins caisses populaires, born in 1900 Québec out of Catholic mutual aid circles, grew into the largest credit union federation in North America. Religious economics at continental scale.

And looking smaller, it's also happening in South Irving's living rooms. Esusu/susu, tanda are rotating money pools older than banks. A group of people pool a fixed amount of money together on a regular schedule, and each member takes turns receiving the full sum. It's an informal system rooted in trust, social connectivity, community accountability -- principles Taabir needs to uphold, not just apply a system to.

And this is Texas, where power pools in religion. A big component of the free gathering infrastructure of South Irving is the churches and the mosque. Zakat moves significant capital every year. A nonprofit that can host religious economics by lending at no interest beside a food ministry, partnering with faith institutions without demanding anyone's doctrine, providing faith-aligned aid, is part of welcoming the community by referencing common language. Faith is trust.

## A media arm

The [Minocqua Brewing Company](https://minocquabrewingcompanytimes.substack.com/) in small town Wisconsin funds a progressive local news operation from its taproom. Their commerce is paying for journalism as a public good, no ads, no paywall. 

Taabir already broadcasts some: this garden, the website, the Substack, even social media presence is a form of, well, media. But funding adds accountability: we could get coverage of the city's land bank, the zoning board, the council votes that decide what South Irving gets to keep.

[Dallas Free Press](https://dallasfreepress.com/) does an amazing job of this in South and West Dallas, especially through its [Documenters](https://dallas-tx.documenters.org/) program inviting anyone to get paid for citizen journalism. A big endorsement from me as a former writer with them. 

## A consultancy

Session 18's whole closing move is that not everyone needs to found something. There are different ways to do something good. Some people build, or teach, or fund. A cooperative consultancy is something that multiplies while keeping the dream alive through payroll. 

Taabir Fellows should look in this direction. A consultancy that exports the model is the one that can fulfill all the dreams on this page and more.

## The tension underneath

Some of these dreams are for the dreamers, right? But we're also building for the people who live in Irving. 

Taabir is this Next Economy organization, radical by any grantmaker's taxonomy, meant to serve a population that is conservative in its own way. Here we are in North Texas. 

But the overlap in values is real: thrift, self-reliance, no debt, tithing, feeding your neighbor, suspicion of large institutions, family obligation as economics. That is the solidarity economy's toolkit, and it's a house and a fellowship hall and a temple. The left calls it mutual aid and sometimes acts like they invented it.

This list is, naturally, incomplete! Bring a service, ask the question: what does a free version of this look like?, and you have a project.
