# The Taabir Fellows

The Taabir staff experience is currently framed as a Fellowship. We are students. The startup provision ([[Bylaws#Startup Provision]]) allows streamlined governance while we are still small enough to know each other's work intimately.

## The Fellows

### Hadron Chaudhary | Keeps Our Cool | Welcome to my Garden ;)

Nineteen years ago, Rachael Maddox wrote a thesis that brought Cooperative Housing at University of Maryland (CHUM) into existence, a 40-member intentional living community where residents developed shared resources, responsibilities, and governance, the same kind of cooperative spirit at the heart of TAABIR. Eight years ago, Hadron Chaudhary was President of CHUM, stewarding an organization that had already outlived its founder's college years, doing everything from going to tenant court to push back against predatory landlords to taking the bus across town to get groceries for everyone in the house. Since then, Hadron has developed a career in environmental sciences and worked for the U.S. EPA, the Dallas Free Press, and the waste management and carbon removal industries. Taabir is an extension of the desire to build with other people, rooted in a love for Dallas and a belief that the community and the organization will be shaped by each other.

### Ramsha Rahim | Gets Shit Done

Ramsha Rahim knows what it means to start over. Having built a career from the ground up across Pakistan and the United States, she brings hard-won experience in operations, strategy, and marketing to her work at TAABIR. She is drawn to TAABIR because she understands from lived experience what it feels like to navigate systems that were not built with you in mind. As an immigrant who has faced her share of hard chapters, she knows what it means to keep going anyway. As a TAABIR Fellow and LIFT Economy MBA participant, she is contributing to the development of TAABIR's vocational school and resource center in Dallas, with a particular commitment to supporting immigrants and others finding their footing in an unfamiliar system. She brings the same drive and follow-through to community building that she has brought to every room she has ever walked into, and is currently pursuing a Doctorate in Business Intelligence, committed to building things that last and anchoring her work in the long project of building economies that actually serve people.

### S. Wazir Ali, M.Sc. | Lights a Fire Under Our Asses

S. Wazir Ali is dedicated to transmuting revolutionary energy into the co-creation of a new world. With over a decade of experience in political organizing and clinical research, she specializes in strengthening the infrastructure between community groups and the solidarity economy. As a consultant with Upside Down Consulting, she discovered a passion for shifting from political resistance toward the active imagination and creation of community-led systems. She holds an M.Sc. from Tufts Medical School, where she studied the biological impacts of historical trauma to anchor social policy in the logic of community resilience. Now, as a TAABIR Fellow and LIFT Economy MBA participant, she serves as a lead architect for the TAABIR vocational school and resource center, building a Dallas ecosystem rooted in participatory governance and worker ownership.

## The Community Connector

### Julio Acosta

Julio Acosta was born in Mexico. His dad is from El Salvador and his mom is from Mexico. His family moved to Dallas in 1987. Since 2015, he has been involved with advocacy, activism, community organizing, and progressive electoral politics. He has served as Community Organizer, Campaign Manager for a Texas State House Representative race, and Deputy Campaign Manager for a progressive U.S. Congressional race in Dallas. Julio enjoys sci-fi, rom-coms, and thriller movies. Some of his favorite shows include Community, The IT Crowd, and Stranger Things.

As Community Liaison for TAABIR USA, Julio is in charge of helping build and maintain bridges with community stakeholders. His role is the primary engine for Taabir's movement building: embedding the organization in West Irving through relational presence rather than traditional outreach, and partnering with the City of Irving on a model of service delivery based on partnership rather than a funder-grantee dynamic. The Community Connector role went to someone who had already shown up, unprompted, at a Taabir community gathering. The real test was lived observation, not a panel interview.

Contact Julio at jacosta@taabirusa.org.

## Advisor

### Tlaloc Fierro

Tlaloc, a Dallas native and community leader, brings a perspective shaped by personal experience. Having faced homelessness after coming out at the age of 17, they are deeply committed to empowering the community through authentic connections. With over a decade of experience in personal and commercial insurance, they have honed a talent for building relationships while assisting clients with risk management and asset protection. Discovering this career path while navigating homelessness, they recognize the transformative power of stability and financial security.

As an advocate for youth, they have served as co-chair of the Metro Dallas Youth Committee, a youth-informed network dedicated to reducing homelessness among young people. Additionally, they have contributed as a Steering Committee member and advocacy team lead for After8toEducate, a program supporting unsheltered youth in DISD high schools and addressing the immediate needs of homeless young adults. Tlaloc is the founder and host of Cereal & Civil Rights, a casual public forum for discussing local issues in Dallas. They are also a key member of W.A.T.E.R., a team of community volunteers mobilizing during local natural disasters, established during Winter Storm Uri. Tlaloc graduated from Dallas College in 2022 with an Associate of Arts with an emphasis in Peace and Human Rights and is currently pursuing a Bachelor's in Business Administration from the University of Texas at Dallas.
