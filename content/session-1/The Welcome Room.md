# The Welcome Room

The soul of Taabir is a free, welcoming place to simply belong without being asked to buy anything or prove anything. Every other possible function, the classrooms, the desks, the café, the market, is built outward from this room. The first thing Taabir offers is free.

"Free" means two things here, and the tension between them is the central design problem of the entire organization.

## Free at the Door

The Welcome Room is a passive, non-transactional space. A place to exist, to be human without consumption attached. No eligibility screening, no intake form, no proof of income, no documentation. You walk in, you sit down, you are welcome.

The free room has to come first, because without it the question, "What do you need?" is not even available to ask.

The room is [[Built From Absence]]. A free, no-conditions gathering space exists because someone knows that cost, financial or emotional, the cost of having to prove you deserve to be there, is a barrier that excludes exactly the people who need a place to sit. It is an echo in conversations across DFW.

> **West Irving Library.** People come for the space because it is free, and they do not need to do anything to belong there. People like a place where they can do nothing, hang out late, and charge their devices. Programming does not require a library card and they see strong usage from children, teens, and adults. The library makes no money of its own and is entirely dependent on city funding. As a primary resource, the library is a designated cooling center and distributes free water when the city supplies it. When the temperature peaked around 113 degrees, the free water distribution was not deemed necessary by the city yet. The library cannot even buy its own water to give out.
>
> *Field Notes, [[Field Notes - What the Surveys Don't Catch]]*

That library is the highest-rated and most-used city resource across all Irving zip codes, with satisfaction above 80 percent in the city's own survey data. People did not say they love the library because it has books. They use it because it is a place to simply be. That is the Welcome Room described by someone who does not know they are describing it.

## Free from Masters

But a room needs a roof, and a roof costs money. "Free" also means the room can afford to exist on its own terms. Revenue is what protects the Welcome Room's ability to be free at the door.

This is the tension the team is sitting in right now. Funders ([[Bylaws#Fiscal Sponsor Relationship]]) want itemized costs, building layout, and a clear funding ask. That is a reasonable request from someone holding the legal and financial structure. It also means the organization needs to demonstrate, in numbers, how a free room pays for itself.

The answer is that it does not pay for itself alone. The Welcome Room is free. Something next to it is not.

## The Local Goods Market

The first space Taabir opens will likely be a rented retail space, roughly 2,000 square feet. Smaller than the phased ownership vision in the [[Site Strategy#Sequencing Logic|site strategy's phased vision]], because ownership comes later. The room does not need to wait for the perfect building. It needs a door and a floor and someone there to hold it open.

The layout is two things side by side:

**The Welcome Room itself.** Free seating, a place to rest, restrooms, makerspace materials, someone present who knows the community and can connect people to resources. The switchboard function lives here. Taabir will find ways of need-meeting without cash changing hands ([[Session 2 - Intro to Next Economy II|Session 2]]).

**The local goods market.** A retail floor where people can sell things from home businesses, where Taabir sells goods from cooperatives and takes a consignment cut. Something to anchor the solidarity economy. It gives home businesses a physical storefront they cannot afford on their own. It gives pop-up sellers permanence, a floor under their feet instead of a tent that folds up on Sunday. It gives cooperatives a retail channel. The consignment model means Taabir earns revenue by connecting producers to customers, keeping money circulating locally, and taking a transparent, mission-aligned cut for perpetuating something the community uses.

The [[Economic Zones of Transaction|four zones of transaction]] describe how needs get met. The market operates in Zones 2 and 3: barter and local cash, money that stays in the neighborhood and keeps circulating. The Welcome Room operates in Zone 1: gift, something given with no expectation of equivalent return.

## Revenue and Independence

Here is why the revenue side matters beyond just paying rent. A room that is free at the door but dependent on any single funder is not actually free. It is free at the point of use and captive at the point of funding. If the funder asks questions about who uses the space, if the grant reporting requires documentation of the people walking through the door, the room's ability to hold undocumented immigrants in confidentiality starts to erode. The room's ability to serve anyone, no questions asked, depends on not having to answer to someone who might ask.

Earned revenue from the market is what sets Taabir free to hold that line.

Revenue does feel less charming than free. The charm of the Welcome Room is that it asks nothing of you. The charm of the market is harder to feel because it involves money changing hands, percentages, inventory, the mechanics of commerce. But the market is what makes the room possible, and the room is what makes the market worth having.

## The Switchboard

The Welcome Room does not stop at the walls of the building. When the room connects someone to a resource outside, a food pantry, a legal clinic, a housing opportunity, a job training program, the need is still being met. It's important to know what resources exist in Irving, to connect with the resource system relationally. Social connectivity is economic connectivity.

## The Room as Message

The Welcome Room is meant to be a marketing channel ([[Session 15 - Marketing]]). Word of mouth starts here, because the room itself is the message. Every person who walks in and finds a place to sit, free, no questions, becomes someone who tells someone else. Every person who finds a home for their club or space for their flyer, a room for their event, that's the actual impact.

The room is also vision discipline ([[Session 7 - Visioning]]). If a proposed function does not serve to welcome the community, it does not get built. Each phase of expansion is a check on whether the next function serves the vision or distracts from it.
