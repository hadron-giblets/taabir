title: "The Anchor Strategy: Learning as Multiplier"
---

[Evergreen Cooperatives](https://www.evgoh.com/) built their model around anchor institutions: hospitals, universities, places too geographically locked to leave a neighborhood even if they wanted to. The move was to redirect their procurement budgets toward worker-owned cooperatives that hire from the same disinvested neighborhood where the institution sits. The strategy starts with the institution, not the people.

Taabir does it the other way, starting with the people. The institutional insight still holds, it just gets applied differently: find something that will persist, that has resources to offer, and build around it. Taabir is trying to become its own anchor, a place where adults learn, practice, fail, and try again with a safety net underneath them. Teaching is the multiplier here. Every person who learns cooperative governance, practices a business model in a low stakes setting, and experiences the solidarity economy as something they have actually done rather than something they have read about carries that knowledge out the door.

Evergreen's structure also points at what a nonprofit partnership could look like for Taabir: a nonprofit holding partial equity in the cooperatives it helps start, a shared services entity that takes administrative weight off those cooperatives, or a development fund that buys and converts existing businesses instead of building new ones from scratch.

**Open questions for Taabir:**
- What would a nonprofit partner actually provide: capital, credibility, expertise, space?
- What would Taabir provide in return: programming, community access, the teaching infrastructure itself?
- Could the partnership eventually hold equity in cooperative businesses the resource center spawns?
- Is there a future where Taabir itself functions as something like a development fund?
