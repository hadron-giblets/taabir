# The Cup of Tea, Turned Upside Down: Being Taught

Every object carries an invisible supply chain. A cup of tea is a map of extraction if you trace it back far enough: monoculture farming, chemical inputs, fossil-fuel transport, packaging waste, water systems built on environmental injustice. The proposed fix is localization. Grow your own. Know your source. Consume things where you can actually see the origin.

For Taabir, the move is to learn this rather than invent it. In this phase, the community teaches Taabir, not the other way around: the needs assessment, the door-to-door canvassing, conversations on porches and at pop-ups. Where do you get your food? Who fixes your car? How do you care for your kids? Where does your grandmother find company? Those questions surface the economy that is already running, the web of gift, barter, and mutual aid that exists outside of GDP measures.

DFW is enormous and well-resourced, so the real question for South Irving is not whether something exists somewhere in the metro. It is whether a resident can reach it, and whether it can reach them. A cup of tea in West Irving might draw from any of the [[Economic Zones of Transaction|zones]]:

- Gift: a neighbor's mint patch, the church kitchen, a grandmother's home remedy.
- Barter: the mechanic who fixes a car because you watch his kids, the seamstress who mends clothes for garden surplus.
- Local cash: the taquería on Irving Blvd, the barbershop that's been there thirty years, the woman selling tamales from her porch on weekends.
- Global supply chain: Walmart, Amazon delivery, fast food.

The community already runs on gift and barter. People here are already economists! We're all juggling a world that got incredibly more expensive in the past 5 years. Taabir is not bringing the solidarity economy to South Irving but naming what is already there, strengthening it, and connecting it to resources the community could not reach on its own.

**Questions for the garden:**
- What does South Irving's existing gift economy actually look like?
- What needs cannot be met locally, and where is the nearest place that can meet them?
- How does Taabir make this invisible economy visible without exploiting or co-opting it?
- What would a real South Irving "cup of tea" map look like?
