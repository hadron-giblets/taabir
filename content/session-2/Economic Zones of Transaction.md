title: "Economic Zones of Transaction"
---

A way of sorting how a need actually gets met, by what kind of exchange is happening:

###### - **Zone 1, Gift:** something given with no expectation of equivalent return. A neighbor's garden surplus, a church meal, a grandmother's home remedy.
###### - **Zone 2, Barter:** a direct trade of goods or labor, no cash involved. A mechanic who fixes a car in exchange for childcare.
###### - **Zone 3, Local cash:** money changes hands, but it stays in the neighborhood and keeps circulating there. A decades-old barbershop, your neighborhood dancehall, someone selling tamales from their porch.
###### - **Zone 4, Global supply chain:** money leaves the neighborhood for a distant, often extractive supply chain. Walmart, Amazon, fast food.

The further an economy moves toward Zone 4, the thinner its mutual-aid networks tend to get (see [[The Density Problem - Scale and Connection|the Inuit whale harvest example]]). Zones 1 and 2 are usually invisible to GDP, but they're real economic activity. A community running on them is already practicing a version of the solidarity economy, whether or not it uses that language for it.
