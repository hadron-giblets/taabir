Four ideas from this session: scale and connection, anchor institutions, the transition between old and new economic forms, and learning from a community before trying to teach it anything.

## The notes

**[[The Density Problem - Scale and Connection|The Density Problem: Scale and Connection]]** — Gift and barter build dense relational networks, and cash thins them — LIFT's example is the Inuit whale harvest collapsing into something much thinner once money arrived. The gathering room is small, but the connections it facilitates don't face the same limitation.

**[[The Anchor Strategy - Learning as Multiplier|The Anchor Strategy: Learning as Multiplier]]** — Evergreen built its model around anchor institutions too geographically locked to leave; Taabir runs the strategy in reverse, starting with the people and becoming its own anchor, with teaching as the multiplier.

**[[Seed and Hospice - Radical Pragmatism|Seed and Hospice: Radical Pragmatism]]** — The Two Loops framework, a compost reframe, and the question that rescues business-as-usual revenue: does this source feed something beyond Taabir's own operation?

**[[The Cup of Tea, Turned Upside Down - Being Taught|The Cup of Tea, Turned Upside Down: Being Taught]]** — Every object carries a supply chain of extraction, and the localization fix for Taabir is learning the economy that already runs South Irving rather than inventing one. People here are already economists.

**[[Bioregionalism - Import, Export, Absence|Bioregionalism: Import, Export, Absence]]** — DFW's pattern isn't scarcity but export: sun, soil, and treated water produced here and shipped away. Where a building's own footprint could close smaller loops.

**[[Economic Zones of Transaction]]** — The supporting concept for the whole session: gift, barter, local cash, and global supply chain as a four-zone sorting of how a need actually gets met.
