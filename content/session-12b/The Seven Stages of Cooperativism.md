# The Seven Stages of Cooperativism

Learning to participate in shared-power governance is a developmental arc, not a switch that flips once someone joins a cooperative structure. 

The seven stages: 
silence, not trusting the process enough to participate at all; 
cautious input, testing whether voice actually matters; 
everything counts, weighing in on everything including pizza toppings; 
overcorrection, meetings stretching long because cooperation feels like it requires exhaustive input; 
discouragement, the sense that this doesn't work; 
discernment, learning which decisions actually need a person's voice and which don't; 
and finally facilitation, holding space for other people's journey through the same stages.

With three team members, Taabir is early in this arc. The bylaws' Startup Provision allows streamlined governance while staff stays under six, which is itself a recognition that small teams need a different kind of muscle than large ones, and that forcing full-stage governance prematurely would produce stage four exhaustion without the benefit stage six is supposed to deliver.
