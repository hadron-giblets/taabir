# Simple Mechanism, Hard Culture: Consent vs. Hierarchy

Shared-power governance is mechanically simpler than corporate hierarchy. No Robert's Rules, no parliamentary procedure, no chain of VP approvals. But it's culturally harder, because it asks people to build muscles most of them were never trained to build: distinguishing a preference from an actual harm, making a proposal imperfect on purpose so others have room to improve it, and sitting patiently through the slow, everything-takes-forever middle stages on the way to something faster and more trustworthy on the other side.

The bylaws' consent process, no one has a valid objection, and preferences are not objections, is mechanically simpler than any corporate decision chain could be. There's no approval ladder to climb. But that simplicity puts more weight on the team's ability to actually tell the difference between "I don't love this" and "this would cause real harm," and that distinction has to be practiced, not assumed.
