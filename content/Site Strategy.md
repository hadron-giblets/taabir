The comprehensive site strategy for Taabir's first permanent home in West Irving (75061), focused on phased development and mission-aligned acquisition. Originally distilled from the Taabir Property Plan for City Collab (June 2026, living document v2) and refined through the course work in sessions 2, 5, 6, and 7.

## West Irving Context

West Irving is a young community with a median age around 32 and median household income near $32,000. It has a strong Asian population, a historic and underserved Black neighborhood (the old Sowers community), and a high concentration of Latinx and Asian immigrant families. The area is physically isolated by highways, has aging building stock, and thin community resources. The community made it clear: what's needed is not another program, but a place to simply belong [2].

## Acquisition Pathways

Six potential acquisition pathways, ordered by mission alignment and speed:

### Irving Redevelopment Land Bank (Featured)
City conveys property specifically to advance equity and community benefit. Proposals are scored partly on community meeting space.

- Cost: Below-market acquisition with community-benefit commitments required in return
- Timing: Annual cycle, next window around January 2027
- Current inventory: Heritage/Downtown area, adjacent to (not inside) the 75061 core

### Direct Purchase + CDFI Mortgage
Buy on the open market with a nonprofit-friendly lender like Nonprofit Finance Fund, LISC, or Clearinghouse CDFI. Up to 80% LTV.

- Cost: 75061 Class B/C buildings roughly $100–$150/sf list, 20–30% down plus closing
- Timing: Months, depends on board readiness and lender

### Religious Facility Acquisition or Lease-Share
Partner with a faith institution selling, downsizing, or willing to host a tenant.

- Cost: Local comp is around $133/sf for a 5,624 sf facility
- Timing: Faith communities move at their own pace

### Co-Location with Existing Institution
Sublease or partnership with a library, school, nonprofit, or community center.

- Cost: Lowest cost path, typically sublease rate plus light buildout
- Timing: Fastest to open, weakest control

### Seller Financing / Lease-to-Own
Negotiate directly with a motivated owner of an aging building.

- Cost: 10–20% down typical
- Timing: Depends on finding the right seller

### New Markets Tax Credits (NMTC) Layer
Federal program that reduces effective cost on commercial development in qualifying low-income census tracts. This is a financing layer that can stack with most other pathways.

## The Irving Redevelopment Land Bank

The most mission-aligned mechanism we've found. The city acquires and repurposes properties expressly to advance equity and resiliency, reduce blight, and create greener, more resilient community spaces. The program scores proposals on sale price, tax revenue, jobs, and explicitly on community benefits like community meeting space and public art.

The current cycle ran annually, with proposals due in April 2026. The next cycle is anticipated around January 2027. Our strategy is to position for that cycle by getting on the program's contact list, attending pre-proposal meetings, and requesting the Redevelopment Land Bank Program Policy so our proposal is ready rather than rushed.

### Case Study: 100 Rock Island Road
A city-controlled parcel offered specifically for community-benefiting redevelopment. This serves as a model for the diligence we'll run on any candidate: confirm zoning, square footage, building condition, parking and access, and the community-benefit commitments the city would expect in return.

## Sequencing Logic

The governing principle is simple: start helping people first, let revenue and program layer in behind it, and never let the build outrun the community. We open the welcoming room as early as possible. Everything that costs more and permits harder comes later, once the place is already alive [2].

### Phase 0 — Prepare
Community Coordinator embeds; needs assessment; zoning pre-application; land-bank positioning; minimum board stood up; acquisition capital lined up.

- Cost: $25k–$75k (staff, consultants, pre-app, Better Block pop-up)
- Timing: De-risk before we spend, move at the community's pace

### Phase 1 — Open the Doors
Free welcoming room plus restrooms plus one flexible multipurpose room. This is the soul, opening fast. A place to simply be.

- Cost: $100k–$240k (cosmetic plus light remodel, roughly $30–$60/sf on 4,000 sf, plus ADA path-of-travel)
- Permitting: Change-of-use Certificate of Occupancy (lowest-intensity use)

### Phase 2 — Earn and Activate
Co-working desks plus classrooms; vocational training begins once the vocational lead is hired. Daily activity and the first earned revenue.

- Cost: $200k–$420k (moderate buildout, roughly $60–$100/sf on 3,500 sf; furniture and tech extra)
- Permitting: CO covers business/educational use; confirm zoning permits it

### Phase 3 — Anchor and Nourish
Café plus dedicated quiet or spiritual rooms. A gathering anchor and a second revenue stream.

- Cost: $180k–$400k (café at roughly $150–$300/sf on 800 sf including hood, grease, FOG; quiet rooms cosmetic)
- Permitting: Health permit plus grease and FOG; possible added zoning allowance

### Phase 4 — Perform and Power
Performance or black-box space; on-site solar. Highest-cost, highest-infrastructure items come last.

- Cost: $200k–$525k (assembly upgrade $80–$150/sf on 2,000 sf plus sprinklers and egress); solar equals $0 capex via PPA
- Permitting: Assembly upgrades; solar via a third-party PPA

## Sustainability Menu

Our ecological commitment is in the bylaws, and a retrofit of an existing building is itself the most sustainable single move we can make. Reusing structure beats new construction.

### Building Shell and Energy
- Air sealing and insulation, Phase 1
- LED lighting plus occupancy and daylight sensors, Phase 1
- HVAC zoning and smart controls, Phase 1–2
- Cool or reflective roof on re-roof, opportunistic
- Daylighting, skylights, solar tubes, light shelves in interior rooms, Phase 1–2

### Water
- Low-flow fixtures across the board, Phase 1
- Rainwater harvesting for irrigation, Phase 1–2
- Native or drought-tolerant landscaping (xeriscape), Phase 1–2
- Greywater for landscape irrigation, Phase 2–3

### Indoor Air and Materials
- Low-VOC paints, sealants, adhesives, and flooring, Phase 1
- Reclaimed and salvaged materials where structural, any phase
- FSC-certified and locally sourced wood and finishes, any phase
- High-MERV filtration on HVAC, Phase 1–2

### Site and Landscape
- Pollinator-friendly native plantings, Phase 1
- Edible landscape or community garden, Phase 2–3
- Shade trees, Phase 0–1
- Bike racks and walking-route improvements, Phase 0–1
- EV-charging conduit and panel capacity, Phase 2

### Energy Generation
- On-site solar via a third-party Power Purchase Agreement (PPA), Phase 4

## Responsible Stewardship

We have written guardrails against failure modes directly into our binding governance:

- Founder burnout: Generational patience; burnout named as structural failure; redundancy of critical functions
- Power quietly concentrating: Participatory governance; make power plain; every member has bank-account access; annual redundancy audit
- Money becoming opaque: Transparent financial practices; Financial Management Team plus Board Treasurer; regular financial reports; consent-based budgeting
- Mission drift: Consent-based decisions tested against stated values; Board fiduciary backstop
- Pay inequity or runaway cost: 2:1 highest-to-lowest pay-ratio cap and living-wage floor, with time-limited exception for lean times
- Assets leaking out at the end: Dissolution clause that returns assets to the solidarity-economy movement
