title: "Hiring as Invitation, Not Extraction"
---

The session's recruiting principles: hire for the specific role as it's actually described, build relationships with communities before a candidate is ever needed, favor a large pool over a narrow one, use standardized questions so comparisons stay fair, and let community decision-making carry weight alongside individual preference. Critically, hire for values alignment first. A candidate doesn't need to already be fluent in next economy language. Onboarding can teach that. What can't be taught as easily is whether someone's actual values point the same direction as the organization's.

Taabir's growth from three team members to four was a working example. The Community Connector role went to someone who had already shown up, unprompted, at a Taabir community gathering. The real test wasn't a panel interview. It was lived observation: does this person connect naturally with the community, do they enjoy the work, do they keep showing up. That's a contrast with business-as-usual hiring, which tends to extract maximum value while investing minimally in teaching someone a role, and which treats the freedom to grow into a job as something to perform in an interview rather than something the job will actually deliver.

Underneath this is the bylaws' abundance practice: not making hiring or any other decision from a place of scarcity or fear, even though scarcity thinking is often an ingrained instinct rather than a deliberate choice. Treating a new hire as a person to grow alongside, rather than a resource to extract from immediately, is that practice applied concretely. And it has a structural implication too: existing team members hold no more formal power than the newest one. A person whose role or compensation is under discussion gets to be part of that discussion, not have it decided about them.
