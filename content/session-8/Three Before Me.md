title: "Three Before Me"

Among the values circulating for Taabir's bylaws — honesty, grace, mutual aid, justice — one candidate is different in kind: **three before me**. The others name a feeling or a principle. This one names a behavior. It can be checked. It can be failed.

The rule as it lives in mutual aid practice: before you ask the organization for something, try three other things first. Ask a neighbor. Look it up. Attempt the version of it you can do alone. The organization stays the safety net, not the first resort. Flip it for the giving side and it becomes: before doing for someone, offer them three ways to do it themselves or with their people — because help that arrives unrequested and unneeded is sometimes just a small occupation.

Why would an organization whose whole brand is free-with-no-questions write a *try-three-times-first* rule into its own covenant? Because the two rules govern different doors. The Welcome Room's door has no gate — you belong before you perform. But the door marked *organizational resources* — staff hours, money, the lending circle, the moving of furniture — is a different door, and ungated it fails in a specific, well-documented way: three staff burn out absorbing every request, and the "free room" quietly becomes a service desk with unlimited hours. Three-before-me is burnout protection written as a value. It pairs with the bylaws' own line that burnout is a structural failure, not an individual weakness ([[Bylaws#Generational Patience]]). The value is the structure.

It is also a values-made-visible test case, the exact exercise from [[Session 8 - Culture, Values, Team & Compensation|Session 8]]: what does "mutual aid" look like, behaviorally, when two people hold it differently? One person's mutual aid is *give immediately, no questions*. Another's is *build the neighbor's capacity first*. Both are living the value honestly, and the behaviors collide at the moment someone asks for something the organization could give. Three-before-me is a pre-negotiated answer to that collision: the community's own capacities get three chances before the organization's do. It converts a Phoenix-fight about "for the children" into a sequence everyone agreed to in advance.

There is a harder reading, and it should be said plainly because this garden does not file the tension off. Three before me can shade into *prove your need three times before you deserve help* — which is the intake form again, the confession economy, the exact thing the Welcome Room refuses ([[The Free Version of Everything]]). A rule meant to protect the organization from unlimited demand can drift, without anyone deciding it should, into a rule that makes the already-hesitant hesitate longer. The person who took an hour to approach the librarians ([[Conditions, Not Content]]) will read *try three things first* as *you are asking too soon* — every time. So the honest version of this value comes with a counterweight written into it: the three attempts are a practice for growing capacity and networks, not a tollbooth. If someone skips all three and just asks, the answer is still yes. The value governs the asker's practice, never the giver's gate.

That is the version worth writing into a covenant: three before me, as discipline for ourselves; never as a test for someone else.
