# Behavioralizing Values

General values -- community, service, doing it for the children -- feel aligned right up until they meet a specific decision. Two people can hold the identical value and land on opposite behaviors, and no amount of restating the value will resolve that gap. The actual work isn't agreeing on the value. It's behavioralizing it: asking what the value looks like in practice, across different people's frames of reference, before a real conflict forces the conversation to happen under pressure.

Taabir's consent-based process is already a behavioral definition rather than a values statement. "Preferences are not objections" does not ask everyone to want the same thing. It defines, precisely, what counts as a legitimate block: only a proposal that would actively harm Taabir or a community it serves, violate a stated value, or create legal or financial risk. The value, community, is held loosely and open to different interpretations. The behavior, when someone can and can't stop a decision, is defined tightly. This is a structure for disagreement that holds strong so long as the org is small enough that everyone can be fully heard in a discussion. 

What happens as we grow? What happens when there needs to be more organizational considerations, things for the good of Taabir that maybe go against individual behavioralizations and beliefs in values? I confess, I can only ask this abstractly right now. One day we'll find the splitting issue.
